# Preface — Why This Exists

I didn't set out to write a book. I set out to stop rereading the same job posting three times because I couldn't remember if I'd already ruled it out. Somewhere around the fortieth scored role, I realized the thing I'd built to survive my own search was more useful than the search itself. This book is that system, taken apart and handed to you. Every chapter is a piece of the machine: what it does, why I built it that way, and what to steal for yours.

## About Me

I'm a longtime software builder and problem-solver, with a career that's taken me through product management, development, and more job searches than I would have preferred. Having been on both sides of the hiring process (sometimes hiring, sometimes hunting), I've experienced layoffs, pivots, and reinventions from inside and outside of tech. The system I share in this book came from necessity: after hundreds of applications, I needed a way to manage the wear and tear of searching, and to bring structure and sanity to a process that can erode both.

I'm not a career coach or an expert with easy answers. I'm someone who's been through this gauntlet repeatedly, learned a lot the hard way, and built tools to make it more survivable for myself, and, I hope, for you.

Above all, I see myself as a software artisan: I design things, build things, constantly experiment and improve. But nothing has mattered more than learning to automate the parts of life and job searching that drain our time and energy. That's what I want to share with you.

That sharing is also how I think about mentorship. Mentor from inside the craft, not above it. Stay in the work. Teach method, not costume: the load-bearing patterns (how to score a role, how to clear fog, how to leave a trail someone else can fork the same day), not a vibe or a pep talk. Force articulation before answers. Evangelize by shipping; don't hoard the tools. This book is that practice applied to the search itself: clear the fog, hand over the backpack, and help you see the work the way I see it.

## What this book is

Part memoir, part manual. A field guide to running a job search like a software project: dealbreakers, a scoring rubric, a daily sweep, machine and human report formats, outreach that sounds like you, resume tailoring without starting from zero every time, and a system that remembers what you already decided.

It is for people who have been burned by volume: too many tabs, too little judgment left, and no durable way to know whether a role is a yes, a maybe, or noise. If you can keep a checklist and fork a repo, you can use this. You do not need to be a platform engineer. You need to be willing to treat the search as a process you design, not a mood you endure.

Every chapter is a piece of the machine: what it does, why I built it that way, and what to steal for yours. The companion site and repo are meant to be forked, not admired.

## What this book is not

Not a growth-hack listicle. Not "ten LinkedIn tricks that changed my life." Not a promise that automation replaces judgment. The system clears fog and protects attention; you still decide. It will not write your values for you, negotiate your offer, or make a bad company good.

It is also not a guarantee of a job. I built this during an active search. The numbers, the fatigue, and the quiet weeks are real. A good recruiter (human or home-built) still loses sometimes. What it can do is keep you from losing for stupid reasons: forgetting what you already ruled out, rewriting the same outreach from scratch, or scoring with a different gut every morning.

## How to read it

In order if you want the arc: problem, dealbreakers, rubric, sweep, reports, outreach, resumes, memory, quiet markets, limits, fork, close.

Or jump to your current pain. Every chapter is written to stand alone. Stuck in tab hell? Start at chapter 1. Already know your dealbreakers? Skip to the rubric. Ready to build? Chapter 11 is the weekend plan. The templates in the repo are the same artifacts the chapters describe; fill them with your constraints, not mine.

## A note on honesty

Everything here is sanitized but real. Company names get redacted where they would turn this into gossip. The numbers are the numbers: scores, volumes, quiet stretches. I do not invent wins to make the system look smarter than it is, and I do not hide the parts where judgment still has to do the hard work.

If something in these pages disagrees with your market, your seniority, or your dealbreakers, trust your constraints. Fork the method. Leave the costume.
