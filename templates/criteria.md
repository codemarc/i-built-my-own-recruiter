# Criteria Worksheet (Template)

Sanitized template from [Chapter 3](../book/03-define-what-great-looks-like.md). Rank your
categories from #1 (highest priority) down, and write a paragraph — not an adjective — for
what "great" looks like in each. This document feeds the [rubric](rubric.md).

| # | Category | "Great" looks like (paragraph, not adjective) |
|---|----------|-----------------------------------------------|
| 1 | *(e.g., Compensation & Sustainability)* | Floor $___, target $___, strong-yes $___, cash vs. equity preference, benefits stance. |
| 2 | *(e.g., Autonomy & Schedule Control)* | Outcome-measured, no time-audit, schedule split OK, no disclosure requirement for outside obligations. |
| 3 | *(e.g., Mutual Commitment from Leadership)* | Peer-in-the-foxhole vs. sponsor-from-above; matching work ethic; substantive vs. metric-only management. |
| 4 | *(e.g., Cultural Fit)* | Founder-led vs. committee; overhead tolerance; specific cultural filters that would create friction. |
| 5 | *(e.g., Side-Project Coexistence)* | Explicit tolerance for your disclosed side role; structural flexibility; no exclusivity or IP-assignment conflict. |
| 6 | *(e.g., Role Shape & Daily Practice)* | The daily craft you're here to do — three-sentence description of the shape of the work. |
| 7 | *(e.g., Work Worth Doing)* | Mission filter; specific quality signals; hard moral no's. |
| … | *(add or remove categories as your context requires)* | |

## Guidelines

- **Rank cold.** Not against a role you already want. Not on a good day. Not on a bad day.
- **Paragraphs, not adjectives.** A tired version of you at 8pm on day 40 should return the same answer twice.
- **Include the trade-offs.** Which criterion wins when it conflicts with which — write that down.
- **Honor the two-truths problem** if you have one (pragmatic now vs. longer arc, family vs. career, health vs. ambition).
- **Revisit after a thin sweep** (see [Chapter 10](../book/10-when-the-market-goes-quiet.md)). Criteria that produced no matches for three weeks are a hypothesis, not scripture.
- **Do the interview with friction, not compliance.** AI works if you tell it to push back — otherwise it nods.

## How this feeds the rubric

Each ranked criterion maps into the [scoring rubric](rubric.md) as follows:

- The **category** becomes a scored dimension.
- The **priority order** informs the weight (higher-ranked criteria get more of the 100 points).
- The **"great" paragraph** becomes the max-score anchor for that dimension.
